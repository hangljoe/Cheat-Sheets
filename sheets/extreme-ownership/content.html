Win the war within

The core idea

No bad teams, only bad leaders.

Own every outcome in your world. No excuses, no blame. When the plan fails, the leader failed and fixes it.

Standards are what you tolerate, not what you preach.

  • First question: what did I do, or fail to do?
  • Admit it, fix it, build the plan so it can't recur.
  • Credit goes to the team. Failure is yours.

Prioritize & Execute

  1. Detach. Step back, breathe, look around.
  2. Pick the single highest priority.
  3. Say it simply to the whole team.
  4. Put every resource on it.
  5. Move to the next priority.
  6. Re-prioritize the moment the picture changes.

When everything is urgent, nothing is. The leader who stays detached sees the one thing that matters.

Team and communication

Decentralized Command

  • Teams of 4–6 per leader, no more.
  • Everyone knows the intent: what and why.
  • Junior leaders decide inside clear boundaries.
  • Decisions flow down, situational awareness flows up.
  • Trust runs both ways and is earned, not assigned.

Lead down and up the chain

Down

  • Explain the why, not just the order.
  • Keep it simple. Can they repeat it back?
  • Show how their task fits the mission.

Up

  • Push situational awareness upward.
  • Don't ask "what do I do"; propose.
  • Once decided, own the boss's call.

No support from above? Blame yourself first: what haven't you explained?

Execution and performance

Planning checklist

  • Analyze the mission: the why.
  • List people, assets, time.
  • Decentralize the planning.
  • Choose the simplest course of action.
  • Let key leaders plan their part.
  • Plan for likely contingencies.
  • Mitigate every controllable risk.
  • Delegate parts of the plan.
  • Check and question until launch.
  • Brief everyone who takes part.
  • Debrief after. Lessons into the next plan.

The leader's mindset

Believe. Don't buy the mission? Ask up the chain until you do. Then sell the why down.

Check the ego. It clouds judgment. Admit mistakes, take criticism, stay humble.

Decide amid uncertainty. 100 % certainty never comes. Act on the best picture you have, adjust fast.

Discipline equals freedom. Fixed routines free your attention for the unexpected.

Clarity before speed. Don't rush to look decisive. Everyone understands the goal before you move.

Aggressive, not reckless. Don't wait for perfect information or ideal timing. Move fast, but smart.

Trust, but verify. Assume people want to succeed. Give them room to act, check results, give feedback.

Growth and balance

Growth and balance

  • Training is the leader's job. A team that fails was not trained; that is on you.
  • Repetition builds discipline. Practice turns knowledge into instinct, and instinct into freedom under pressure.
  • Humility. Even when you lead, you are still a student: learn from mistakes, subordinates, competitors.
  • Detachment. When emotions rise, step back and look at the situation from above before reacting.
  • No victim mindset. No "because of them" or "the system". Look for what you can influence.
  • Teach ownership. Help others take responsibility and grow into it.

Mission clarity: five questions

  1. What is the mission?
  2. Why does it matter?
  3. Who owns what?
  4. What does success look like?
  5. What are the key risks and contingencies?

Slow is smooth, smooth is fast

Slow is smooth, smooth is fast

Slow is smooth: take the time to do it right. Deliberate and focused beats rushed; haste skips steps and loses control.

Smooth is fast: once a task is practised until it is precise, speed follows on its own, without forcing it.

Leadership is simple, but not easy. A good leader has nothing to prove, yet everything to prove.

Phrases that signal ownership

  • "I own that."
  • "What can I do to help us fix it?"
  • "Let's simplify this."
  • "What's our priority right now?"
  • "Do you clearly understand why we're doing this?"

Failure modes

  • Blame. The moment you blame, you stop fixing.
  • Complexity. A plan nobody can repeat is no plan.
  • Tunnel vision. Fighting every fire at once.
  • Waiting for perfect information.
  • Micromanaging, or vanishing. Both kill initiative.
  • Ego. Defending the plan instead of the mission.
